01 • Welcome to the Swedish capital

Stockholm at a glance

Stockholm combines historic neighbourhoods, museums, modern Scandinavian design, restaurants, parks and water. Central districts have very different characters: Gamla Stan is the historic Old Town, Norrmalm is the busy commercial centre, Södermalm is known for cafés and nightlife, and Djurgården is greener and packed with museums.

Best for history
Gamla Stan, the Royal Palace and Stockholm City Hall.
Best for museums
Djurgården: Vasa Museum, Skansen, ABBA The Museum and more.
Best in December
Christmas lights, Stortorget market, Skansen and traditional Swedish seasonal food.
Winter tip: December days are short and weather can be cold or wet. Warm layers, gloves and waterproof footwear make sightseeing much more comfortable.
02 • Where to stay

Accommodation

Choose an area based on what you want to do. Prices change greatly by season and event dates, so always check current rates and cancellation conditions before booking.

Norrmalm / Central Station
Best for easy transport

Ideal for airport trains, intercity trains, shopping and short stays. Example hotels include Radisson Blu Waterfront, Scandic Continental, Hotel C Stockholm, Freys Hotel and Scandic Grand Central.

Gamla Stan
Best for atmosphere

Stay among medieval streets, historic squares and restaurants. Beautiful in the evening and especially atmospheric around Christmas.

Södermalm
Best for local neighbourhood life

A good choice for cafés, independent shops, viewpoints and nightlife, with quick metro links to the centre.

Djurgården
Best for museums & greenery

Quieter than the city centre and very convenient for museum-focused visits, Skansen and winter walks.

Accommodation examples are orientation suggestions, not a ranking. Check current availability and prices directly with the property or booking service.

03 • Essential Stockholm

Places of interest

Djurgården

Vasa Museum

See the remarkably preserved 17th-century warship Vasa. It is one of Stockholm’s most distinctive museum experiences.

Photo: OneHungLow / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
Gamla Stan

Old Town

Walk through narrow streets, Stortorget square, historic buildings, cafés and small shops. This is one of the most rewarding areas to explore on foot.

Gamla Stan

The Royal Palace

The palace has more than 600 rooms and several museums. Visitors can see ceremonial rooms, royal collections and, on many days, the changing of the guard.

Kungsholmen

Stockholm City Hall

Famous for its tower and Nobel banquet halls. The interiors are visited by guided tour; the waterfront park is also worth seeing.

Djurgården

Skansen

Open-air museum and cultural attraction where Swedish history, traditions and seasonal celebrations come together. It is particularly special at Christmas.

Södermalm

Monteliusvägen & viewpoints

Enjoy classic views over Riddarholmen, City Hall and Lake Mälaren. Sunset and blue hour can be especially beautiful.

Citywide

Stockholm Metro Art

Many underground stations function like public art spaces. A metro journey can become part of the sightseeing experience.

Water

Archipelago excursion

If time allows, take a boat trip toward the Stockholm archipelago. Even short ferry journeys give a different view of the city.

Central Stockholm seen from the City Hall tower. Photo: Bengt Nyman / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0).
04 • Taste Stockholm

Where to eat

Stockholm ranges from traditional Swedish restaurants to modern Nordic cooking, bakeries and international cuisine. For a first visit, try at least one traditional meal and one fika break.

Traditional Swedish food in Gamla Stan

Restaurant Tradition, Stockholms Gästabud, Slingerbulten and Kryp In are examples of restaurants where visitors can look for Swedish classics.

Fika

Take a coffee break with a cinnamon bun (kanelbulle) or another pastry. Fika is both a food habit and a social ritual.

What to try

Köttbullar
Swedish meatballs, often served with potatoes, cream sauce and lingonberries.
Gravlax
Cured salmon, commonly served with mustard-dill sauce.
Toast Skagen
Toast topped with a creamy prawn mixture, often with dill and lemon.
Herring
Pickled herring appears in several traditional meals and seasonal buffets.
Kanelbulle
The classic Swedish cinnamon bun — ideal with coffee.
Julbord
At Christmas, restaurants may serve a large Swedish seasonal buffet with herring, ham, meatballs, sausages, cheeses and sweets.
Restaurant tip: In December, popular Christmas dinners and weekend tables can book up early. Reserve ahead when possible.
05 • Arrival & transport

How to get to Stockholm — and how to move around

From Arlanda Airport — Arlanda Express

The express train links Stockholm Central with Arlanda. The published journey time is about 18 minutes to Arlanda South and 19 minutes to Terminal 5.

Airport buses

Several coach operators connect Arlanda with Stockholm. They can be a practical alternative depending on your budget and destination.

By train

Stockholm Central is the main rail hub and connects directly with T-Centralen metro station and Cityterminalen bus terminal.

By ferry

Stockholm has Baltic ferry links and cruise traffic. Check your exact terminal because ports are spread around the city.

Getting around the city

SL operates metro, buses, commuter trains, trams and several ferries. A standard single ticket is valid for 75 minutes; short-stay visitors can also use time-based travelcards. Contactless cards and the SL app are convenient for many journeys.

Useful route: Tram 7 is convenient for Djurgården attractions. Ferry services can also be a scenic way to travel between central waterfront areas.
06 • Christmas in Stockholm 2026

Christmas markets & festive places

Stockholm’s Christmas season generally begins in mid-November. Streets and squares glow with seasonal lights, and markets sell Swedish food, crafts, candles, decorations and gifts. Always re-check event details shortly before visiting because schedules can change.

Stortorget Christmas market in Gamla Stan. Photo: Yarsaf94 / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0).
Gamla Stan

Stortorget Christmas Market

The traditional market in Stockholm’s oldest square is one of the most atmospheric places for Christmas shopping, warm drinks and seasonal snacks.

2026 schedules are updated during the autumn; verify exact opening dates before your visit.

Djurgården

Skansen Christmas Market

A historic Christmas market tradition dating to 1903, with crafts, seasonal food, bonfires and a strong old-Sweden atmosphere.

Confirmed 2026: 27 November–20 December, Friday–Sunday. Fridays/Saturdays 10:00–17:00; Sundays 10:00–16:00.

Djurgården

Rosendals Trädgård

Greenhouses, bakery aromas, wreaths, gifts and winter garden atmosphere. A student design Christmas market with Beckmans College of Design is listed for 6–7 December, 11:00–16:00.

Central Stockholm

#Stockholmsjul Christmas Lights

From around 15 November, central streets and squares are decorated with large light installations. An evening walk through Norrmalm and the centre is an easy, free festive activity.

Norrmalm

NK Christmas Windows

The Nordiska Kompaniet department store has created elaborate Christmas window displays since 1915. It is a classic stop on Hamngatan.

Kungsträdgården

Winter atmosphere

Kungsträdgården is a central meeting place for seasonal activities, winter events and ice skating. Combine it with nearby Christmas lights and NK’s decorated windows.

Christmas flavours to look for

Glögg
Swedish mulled drink, often served with raisins and almonds.
Pepparkakor
Thin Swedish gingerbread biscuits.
Lussekatter
Saffron buns, especially associated with Lucia season.
Julskinka
Christmas ham, a traditional julbord favourite.
Brända mandlar
Roasted or candied almonds commonly sold at winter markets.
Julmust
A Swedish soft drink strongly associated with Christmas.
